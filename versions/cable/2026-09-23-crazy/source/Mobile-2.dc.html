v2.0Growth

Designed for outcomes, not steps. The product blossomed.

I stopped asking users how they did the work, and started asking what outcome they needed, how they were getting there, and whether a better route existed. I kept the workshops frequent, and their insight gradually sharpened. Soon small tweaks to existing elements became full features.

Assurance

Every account and every transaction tested against the bank’s own controls, continuously. Compliance had been sampling a slice of it once a quarter.

Reports

Teams have to review the quality of their own decisions. The product drew the sample, asked the questions and returned the score, ready to put in front of a regulator.

Risk Assessment

The assessment every regulated firm has to produce each year, built in the product rather than a spreadsheet. Scored as you fill it in, locked once approved.

Actions

Every finding, wherever it surfaced, carried an owner and a date. Remediation happened where the finding was, instead of in somebody’s inbox.

The first iteration of Oversight (then Programs) started to run into UI limitations.

These were not technical people, so the start was slow. But the asks grew with the relationship. The roadmap took shape as we went, evolving along with their insights.

v3.0System

A bag of features, rebuilt as one system.

3.0 was design and engineering, end to end. By then I knew what the product had to be, so I could design all of it at once: find the patterns doing the same job in different ways, keep the strongest of each, and extend it to cover every case. Together we rebuilt the information architecture and the code beneath it.

Oversight, a flagship on par with Paper Trails

The sponsor banks wanted Cable inside Cable: the same product pointed at the fintechs they oversee, so they could read those controls the way they read their own.

Top navigation

Oversight had stacked four headers on one screen. I folded them into a single bar that still told you which bank, which fintech and which feature you were in.

This was a big commitment in resources, and a hard sell. But Oversight, our newest and most popular feature, was bursting at the seams more than any other, and with the banks’ interest in it rising, it pushed the redesign through.
