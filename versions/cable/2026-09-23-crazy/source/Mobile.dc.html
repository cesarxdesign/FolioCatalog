v0.0Diagnosis

The product was the manual process, reproduced in software.

The team had built each step faithfully, and neglected the outcomes behind them, the results that mattered. Asking people to change how they work, for a marginal gain, is a tough trade. The product had to create value, not mirror it.

1

The most important work was happening inside an attachment.

The account IDs under review arrived as a CSV uploaded into the chat, and the reviewing happened in there. The product was just how the file got delivered.

Without a blueprint to follow, first iterations relied heavily on instinct, our pilot users, and first principles.

v1.0Hierarchy

I gave the one feature room, and the work a place to happen.

Two things were clear: the product had to grow, and it had to be worth using. So the first step was breaking up the single screen setup that blocked both.

Paper Trails, the whole reason the product existed, got a high level view of all trails, each a card associated with its state, a pattern users already knew.

2

Same feature set. Different value proposition.

Every Paper Trail is a list of account IDs that someone has to review one by one. Those lines came out of the attachment and became a component of their own, sitting inside the trail, one click to copy each. Over 90% of users engaged with them, the most used component in the product.

Then a dedicated view for the work that happened inside each one. Fincrime tooling was new ground, so our pilot partners had little reference for what to ask for. Giving them something worth reacting to changed the quality of what came back.
