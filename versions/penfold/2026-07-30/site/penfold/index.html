Founding Designer, first hire. Mobile and web, from proof of concept to a live pension. 2019/2020, London, onsite.

foundation

Live in a week. Then onboarding cut from 33 steps to 5.

  • Week one: the proof of concept became a live app, in time for the funding round. It shipped fast and ugly: 33 onboarding steps.
  • The first real users signed up, the round closed, and the focus turned to growth.
  • I cut onboarding to 5 independent modules, so users could pause and finish later without losing progress.

design system

I built one mobile-first component set that fed every platform.

  • Desktop and mobile components were nearly identical, so mobile became the single source of truth.
  • Engineering could iterate in isolation, and we shipped daily.
  • Feedback ran on a daily loop: session replays every morning, Customer Support tickets reviewed every Friday, fixes often shipped same-day.

We had optimized speed. Targeting was next, and it was strategy, not design. Penfold sold pensions to people without one: a huge market, and a slow one, because every signup needed educating first. People who already had a pension needed none. They just needed a better pension. Winning them was a product problem, and we were fast at product.

product strategy

Stop selling new pensions: combine existing ones.

  • The battleground changed: from educating users, to winning them on product.
  • The product rebuilt around combining pots people already had, instead of opening an empty one and waiting on a first contribution.
  • Signups rose from ~100 to ~600 a month, each arriving with an existing pot, so signup value rose with volume.

impact

Year one: 3,000 savers and £4M under management.

  • A dozen users in the first week grew to ~3,000 savers and £4M AUM by the end of year one, on plan.
  • The foundation still serves Penfold today: over £1B managed, voted Best Pension 2026.
